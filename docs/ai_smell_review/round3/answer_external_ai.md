## 2. Paragraphs that sound most clearly written by a person

- **P25.** “These documents did not cover Fig. S1, whose runs were made earlier.” This answers the question I would actually ask on seeing a figure about a shift that was supposedly predicted in advance. The detail about chronology earns its place.
- **P26.** “its intervals at h* and at 2h* both reached just above 1.25” tells me exactly how the first test missed. “although four of the seven per-model intervals reached above 1.25” keeps me from mistaking a result about the median for seven individual confirmations.

## 5. Three changes I would make

1. **Give the abstract one principal result and one clearly stated limit.** Its most useful contrast is between “a median over models of 1.04” for the change in the log rate ratio and “that temperature did not always move as intended” for the crossover target. Give that contrast room. Move the step-localization result and details of the larger-model and multiplicative tests out of the abstract's closing run of sentences. Those findings would still be available in the results; a reader of the abstract alone would lose some secondary coverage.
2. **Let the research question organize the literature paragraphs.** In P2, keep what has been tried, but group the cited approaches rather than repeatedly ending sentences with “have been proposed”. In P5, explain which earlier result makes one expect a *future* count to matter here. That would preserve the citations and the distinctions between training, decoding and trajectory weighting. No scientific information needs to be removed.
3. **Name the measured quantity when the prose switches quantities.** The places that need this most are “its median” in P28, the two definitions of s_temp in P20, and the jump from ln K to “the covariance of the switching counts” in P31. Distinguish temperature from its shift, one-temperature ratios from their aggregate, and a combined response from its four components. This costs a few words but removes no result. It would also let the discussion keep its necessary qualifications without explaining the same result again.

## 6. Copy, notation, and consistency check

I found no unambiguous misspelled word. The following are the issues I found in the supplied text. I distinguish an internal wording mismatch from a result that would require the omitted material to check.

**Wording that changes the apparent claim**

- **P1/P7, which penalty acts on which tokens.** The abstract says “The penalty we study adds h to the logits of repeat candidates” and then “The widely used multiplicative penalty followed the prediction”. P7 explicitly gives the multiplicative penalty “a third set, all tokens that have appeared earlier.” The singular “The penalty we study” reads too broadly. Likewise, “the same penalty would produce on whole texts” in P1 is an analogy to the whole-text tilt by exp(hN/T), not a second application of the decoding algorithm. The two comparisons need distinct names.
- **P12/P15, the conditioning convention.** P15 says “write N − E[N] as the sum of updates of the expected final count, E[N | x_≤t] − E[N | x_<t].” This is correct with a fixed prompt and expectation conditional on that prompt. With random articles and a prompt observed before step 1, the sum telescopes to N minus its conditional mean given the prompt. P6 uses multiple prompts and P32 distinguishes “covariances within articles” from “covariances pooled over articles”. The conditioning convention matters to P15's pathwise identity and should be stated there.
- **P20/P32, exact derivative versus estimated slope.** P20 defines “b = ∂ ln K/∂β”; P32 describes “computing s_temp from the step-wise temperature identity instead of the slope b”. Under the exact definition the identity gives that derivative. A slope estimated from several temperatures can instead approximate it. The text shifts between the mathematical quantity and its estimator without marking where.
- **P21/P33, scope of energy invariance.** “The prediction needs an energy for each step and changes with that choice; the exact identities and s_pen do not.” This is clear for the penalty score and for the *centered* temperature score under the history-dependent shifts in P20. It could be read as covering the tilted temperature response too. P33 correctly says “Unlike s_pen, s_temp depends on the energy assigned to a step.” The invariant object should be named explicitly.
- **P25/P27/P29, scope of “Each test”.** “Each test compares the realized change in ln K at h* with the first-order prediction h* g(T)” only describes the additive statistic at h*. Even the first additive test has a 2h* result. P29 says “The multiplicative block is at the predicted divisor θ*”. Likewise “We required the bootstrap interval of the median ratio over models to lie between 0.8 and 1.25.” needs an explicit seven-model scope; P30 specifies “median over the first seven models; mean over the last two”. The tests themselves can be reconciled; the general wording cannot cover all of them.
- **P25/P28, direction versus success at a target.** “Penalizing or favoring the repeat candidates moved T_× in the direction of h, roughly linearly (Fig. S1).” This is easy to read as a general result although P25 later identifies Fig. S1 as earlier runs. “The shift of T_× itself, which a user would target, was larger than intended.” holds for the straight-line estimate in P28; interpolation gives “0.87, slightly below the target”. The unqualified opener in P28 conflicts with the second estimate. Neither result says the direction reversed.
- **P31, scope of the percentage.** “For ln K, the compensator term thus cancels about 99% of the tilted term.” The penalty range “between 0.7% and 1.5% of the tilted response” supports that summary. The temperature result is separately reported as “at most 2.3%”; its survival fraction is also defined using a weighted sum. The sentence should identify the perturbation to which its numerical summary applies.
- **P31, whole response versus component covariances.** “Nearly all of the covariance of the switching counts with N comes through the candidates that each choice makes likely to be chosen later” is stronger than the reported measurement. P14 defines the response of ln K using four expected counts. Near-cancellation for their signed combination does not by itself establish near-cancellation for each count. P16 supplies an identity for Cov(O, Q), not a bound on its size relative to Cov(O, N) for each O.
- **P34/P36, what is being summed.** P34 gives the per-step score variance as “q_t(1 − q_t)/T²”; P36 labels the plotted quantity “share of the variance Σ q_t(1 − q_t)”. The missing factor cancels in a share at fixed T. Since the caption says “for the nine models at the test temperatures”, it matters whether shares were calculated before pooling across temperatures. If the intended quantity is the variance of the unscaled indicator residual, the caption should name it. The text alone does not establish a numerical error.
- **P37/P41, how broadly the tests succeeded.** “In the registered tests, the derivative estimated from unpenalized samples predicted the change in switching” can imply that all registered tests confirmed the prediction. P26 says “The first test, on the 40 articles from which h* had been computed, was inconclusive”. P41 repeats that qualification but says the tests “test whether the first-order prediction holds at h*”, again using additive notation in a discussion that explicitly includes the multiplicative test. An inconclusive test is not evidence that the numerical prediction failed; the mismatch is in how broadly confirmation is claimed.

**Local grammar, referents, and notation**

- **P1:** “the derivative of a text's log-probability with respect to h is (N − Q)/T” is clear once Q is defined; the next claim “Q covaries with the switching statistics almost as strongly as N does” leaves the reader to infer which statistic or weighted combination is meant. This is the same scope issue as P31. “With the derivative estimated from the unpenalized runs of the same test” also makes “the same test” momentarily ambiguous after the abstract has mentioned both a first test and a replication.
- **P8:** “a step enters the repetitive state when the fraction reaches 1/2 and leaves it when the fraction falls below 1/4”. A single step has one label; it is the sequence of labels that enters or leaves a state. “We label each step as repetitive or ordinary from the fraction of repeats in a 32-token window around the step, 16 tokens before it and 15 after.” The count works when the current token is included. The main text leaves the edge-window convention to Appendix A.
- **P10/P14:** P10 first writes “p_t^h(x_t | x_<t)” and then “p_t^h(x_t)”. The second drops the conditioning argument without saying it is implicit. P14 uses “a_t − E_t[a]” and then “a is the derivative of a token's logit”. The additive score is explicitly evaluated at h = 0. The multiplicative passage should likewise specify λ = 0 (θ = 1) for the unpenalized prediction. It should make explicit that a_t evaluates that token-dependent function at the sampled token, whereas E_t[a] averages it over possible tokens. “and E_t the expectation” is grammatical ellipsis for “is the expectation”, though slower to parse.
- **P17:** “Its step probabilities are multiplied by exp(hc_t/T) and by the expected future factor” follows a sentence about the *tilted* ensemble. It is the original, unpenalized step probabilities that the factors multiply to obtain the tilted probabilities. “Its” points to the result of the transformation.
- **P20/P33:** P20 uses both “s_temp = b/b_tilt” and “s_temp = Σ_u g_C(T_u)/Σ_u g_C^tilt(T_u)” for a temperature-specific quantity and its aggregate. P33's “the van 't Hoff slope as a percentage of the response of ln K to exponential tilting by exp(−εU)” can sound like a ratio of unweighted summed slopes, although “summed as defined in Sec. III C” points to the proper weighting. P33 also uses the fragment “Sums over the usable temperatures, unpenalized runs on the 120 articles of the replication (smaller models) and of the test on the larger models.” Its connection to both percentage definitions is hard to parse.
- **P26/P28:** “decided on their mean ratio” is an awkward modifier of “test”. In “its median was 1.3 times the intended shift”, the antecedent appears to be T_× rather than its shift. “The median then became 0.87” omits the scale (times the intended shift). “Some smaller models, GPT-2-XL most of all, and the mean of the two larger models still departed from the target” coordinates models with a mean. The things compared to the target are estimated shifts.
- **P30:** “Ratio: realized change in ln K divided by the first-order prediction, summed over the usable temperatures” appears to sum an already formed ratio, or just its denominator. P25 specifies that both changes are summed before division. This is a consequential modifier placement in an otherwise useful caption.
- **P34:** “For these steps” follows the sentence “This proportionality is an empirical finding, not a consequence of the form of the score.” Its intended antecedent is the earlier bin with q_t between 0.1 and 0.9. Repeating the bin limits would prevent a mistaken reading that the 0.96 applies to every step.
- **P36:** “For steps with repeat candidates, split by the model's probability q_t of choosing one (below 0.1, 0.1–0.9, above 0.9): share of the steps, share of the variance Σ q_t(1 − q_t) and share of the predicted response” is a caption fragment in which “split” can momentarily modify the candidates. The bins classify steps. The colon separates the binning rule from the three plotted shares.
- **P37:** “the signed difference between each choice and the model's own probability of choosing a candidate” treats a token choice as a number. The quantity subtracted from is its zero-or-one candidate indicator c_t.
- **P38:** “each of which changes it only slightly” has the ambiguous antecedent noted in §4; the single-count increment is the easiest claim to interpret.
- **P39:** “With −log p_t at T = 1 as the energy of a step it overstated the effect by about 45%” would benefit from a comma after “step” to mark the end of the introductory phrase. The parenthetical reference “(Appendix H)” does not resolve what summary over models the 45% represents.
- **P41/P42:** “the bootstrap intervals resample articles, not models” assigns the resampling to the intervals; it is the bootstrap procedure that resamples articles to construct them. “They were checked by tests, an independent reimplementation and other AI instances, not line by line by the author” refers jointly to “The code and the derivations”. It leaves open which checks covered the derivations and which covered the code.

I would retain three distinctions that could look like inconsistencies on a quick read. P7 deliberately defines a repeat and a repeat candidate differently. P9's failure of a preselected bimodality criterion can coexist with the two peaks described in P22. And P26's successful interval for the median can coexist with individual intervals extending above the band. None of those requires a correction to the underlying claim.
